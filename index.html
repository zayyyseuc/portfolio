I am a designer exploring spatial computing. My work often begins with conditions that are difficult to perceive directly: cities that appear dense yet lack lived vitality, bodily risks that cannot always be actively reported, emotional distance in remote companionship, ecological relationships that are hard to explain, and information structures waiting to be reimagined in immersive space.

I’m interested in how technology can extend the ways we sense, interpret, and respond to the world. Over the past few years, I have developed projects across machine learning, IoT, VR, Unreal Engine, and multimodal sensing: using facial EMG to explore passive early stroke detection, combining sound and image data to read urban vitality, rethinking file systems through spatial interaction in VR, and translating ecological knowledge and emotional presence into experiential systems.

For me, design is a framework for asking questions, sensing reality, and shaping responsive experiences. I hope to work between computational methods and humanistic concerns, creating systems that help algorithms engage with richer contexts and allow interaction to carry the complexity of bodies, cities, emotions, and environments.
