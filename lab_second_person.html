Personal Brain Circles

An AI that sits inside a small circle of people who care about each other. It answers only from what each person chooses to share, and it says so when it doesn't know.

Node.js · bge-m3 · vectra · DeepSeek PWA · Cloudflare Access Live since May 2026
Observation

People are retreating from broadcast into small circles

The feed rewards performance. The group chat of five rewards being known. Among the people around me, the sense of safety now comes from a handful of people who already have your context: a parent, two friends, a sister in another city, a roommate.

These circles run on scraps: a voice note, a photo of dinner, "did you eat?". They are warm and inconsistent. The person sharing has to keep deciding what to say, and the people who care have to keep asking. Distance and time zones make both harder.

If an AI sits inside a small circle, how should people share information, and share themselves, through it? What should it pass along, what should it hold back, and who decides?

Where it started

A circle of two, across an ocean

Personal Brain began as a retrieval agent over my own notes: a way to ask questions of my past self. The first circle came next. My parent lives abroad and wanted to know how I was doing without asking every day. So I built a second, much smaller index from only the notes I mark for the circle, and a phone app on top of it.

The page below generalizes from that first circle. A circle member here can be a parent, a sibling, a close friend. The design questions are the same.

Analysis

Five channels, ordered by how much the system interprets

A circle member does not get one feed. They get five channels. Each one moves further from words I wrote and closer to the system's reading of me. That shift is where trust has to be designed.

STATUS CARD

One line I write

"Writing up review notes. Tired but good." Shown exactly as typed, with the time it was updated.

MILESTONES

Dates I choose

The system only counts the days left. A shared horizon gives the circle something to look forward to together.

RECENT SHARES

Notes I marked

The three newest shared notes, cut to 150 characters. My words, but the system picks the excerpt.

ACTIVITY HEATMAP

My behavior, counted

Thirty days of how much I used my own tools. I never wrote it. A member reads it as "how she's doing", which it can't tell them.

ASK

AI answers from shared notes

Short, warm, grounded only in the circle index. It is told to say "I'm not sure" instead of guessing.

How a circle should share

Eight design principles

Built running in the current systemProposed next iteration

Private by default, shared by the piece Built

Nothing reaches the circle unless I mark that note public: true. Whole folders, like drafts or a journal, can be excluded so they are never even offered.

The boundary lives in the architecture Built

The circle's AI searches a separate index built only from shared notes. A prompt can be talked around. An index that never contained the note cannot leak it.

Layer the channels Built

Different needs get different channels, from a line I write to an AI answer. Members can stop at whichever layer is enough.

Presence over notification Built

No pushes, no read receipts. Members visit when they think of me. The circle feels like a lit window, not an inbox.

A mediator, not a stand-in Built

The AI speaks as my assistant, never as me. It keeps answers to a sentence or two and admits when it doesn't know.

Point back to the person Proposed

When the AI can't answer, its best move is to suggest asking me directly, with one tap. The mediator should create contact, not replace it.

Everyone shares, not one person Proposed

Today the circle has one sharer. A circle where only one person is visible is an audience. Each member should have their own vault and their own shares.

Label what a signal can't say Proposed

The heatmap should say "activity, not mood" on its face. Any inferred channel needs to show the limit of its inference.

What this design does not resolve yet

Closeness or avoidance

If asking the AI is easier than asking me, the circle might get quieter. The measure of success is more direct contact, not more questions to the AI.

Activity is not wellbeing

The heatmap counts what I do. Members read it as how I feel. A passive signal can comfort, and it can also mislead.

Asking is also sharing

A member's question ("is she sleeping?") reveals their worry. Today those questions sit in a debug log. Members should know who can see what they ask.

Curation is work

Opting in note by note keeps me in control and costs effort. Can the system suggest what to share without deciding for me?

Emotional sovereignty

I won't let an AI decide my mood or taste. Yet here an AI speaks about me inside my closest relationships. The design has to keep that voice small.
