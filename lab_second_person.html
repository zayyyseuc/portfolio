Second Person

An AI interface for communicating with my accumulated self.

Personal AI · Self-initiated Experiment · Local-first RAG · 2026

I built a local-first AI system that turns my personal archive into a conversational space. It helps past experiences, unfinished ideas, and recurring concerns return to my present thinking — not by remembering for me, but by reducing the friction of reaching what I have already lived and thought.

Weekly Reflection · 2025

Perhaps the inconvenience was part of what made the experience feel personal.

I had built an archive I could no longer reach.

Years of project notes, personal essays, and unfinished conversation records had accumulated in my Obsidian vault. The problem stopped being capture a long time ago — it became return.

  • Folder search assumes I remember where a thought was filed, not what the thought was
  • Keyword search assumes I remember how I phrased it at the time — often the hardest thing to recall
  • Most of what I retain is shape, not text: a tension, a mood, a decision that felt like an earlier one
  • Even a located record rarely explains its own relationship to the question I'm asking now

Could I retrieve personal experience through meaning rather than location or exact language?

Turning an archive into a conversational space

Second Person connects a local Obsidian vault to semantic retrieval and a language model. When I ask a question, it searches for conceptually relevant fragments across my archive, brings them into the current context, and helps me examine how my thinking has developed over time.

Three modes of self-communication

Not feature categories — three different reasons I return to the archive.

Recall
Finding what I could no longer name
  • Starts from a vague question, not a precise keyword
  • Semantic retrieval pulls concept-related records across time and files
  • Answers stay traceable to an identifiable personal source, not generic knowledge
Reflect
Noticing patterns across time
  • A weekly review pulls together the past period's records
  • The system surfaces recurring themes, habits, and tensions
  • The output is a reflection question, not a verdict on who I am
Continue
Letting unfinished thoughts develop
  • A half-formed idea can be logged into the Lab feature
  • Continued dialogue develops a fragment into a clearer idea
  • The fuller version moves into Imagination notes or the personal archive

Design Decisions

  • Local-first storage — personal records stay in a vault I can inspect and manage myself
  • Semantic retrieval — relevant records surface without depending on exact keywords
  • Persona file — a written context file gives the model consistent background and interaction rules

 

  • Visible sources — responses stay traceable to which personal traces the model was drawing from
  • Continuous indexing — new writing becomes reachable in future retrieval without a manual rebuild
  • Explicit sharing rules — private and family-facing material are separated by an explicit boundary, not a default

The archive became something I could return to.

Shift 01
From searching for files to expressing uncertainty

I no longer need to translate a question into a precise query — I can start from an unclear question instead.

Shift 02
From storing ideas to revisiting them

A fragment doesn't disappear into a folder the moment it's written down — review and conversation give it repeated chances to return.

Shift 03
From asking an external intelligence to consulting personal evidence

The most useful answers aren't ones that hold more outside knowledge than I do — they redirect me back to my own attention.

The value of the system was not that it knew more than I did. It helped me regain access to what I already knew.

Retrieval is also interpretation.

Similarity scores, chunking boundaries, and the model's own phrasing all shape which past traces get seen — and how they get connected. The system can't stand neutral to "the past." At least three tensions stay unresolved:

Coherence vs. Contradiction

Does the AI smooth over a contradictory, uneven history into a more consistent-sounding identity than I actually have?

Convenience vs. Agency

Does lowering retrieval friction also quietly weaken my own memory and my own interpretive authority over my past?

Relevance vs. Serendipity

As retrieval gets more optimized for relevance, does the value of stumbling onto an unrelated, unexpected record get lost?

The system should not produce a definitive version of who I am. It should surface traces, preserve contradiction, and hand back the conditions for meaning — not the meaning itself.

Current State

A functional personal prototype connected to my active Obsidian vault. The system supports semantic conversation, weekly reflection, idea development, persistent dialogue, automatic indexing, and selective family-facing access.

The live system contains private personal material and is therefore not publicly accessible. This page uses interface recordings and anonymized interaction examples instead.
