Second Person

An AI interface for communicating with my accumulated self.

Personal AI · Self-initiated Experiment · Local-first RAG · 2026

I built a local-first AI system that turns my personal archive into a conversational space. It helps past experiences, unfinished ideas, and recurring concerns return to my present thinking — not by remembering for me, but by reducing the friction of reaching what I have already lived and thought.

Challenge

Capture had succeeded. Retrieval had failed.

Years of notes remained available in theory, but folders and exact keywords made them difficult to reach from a present, half-formed question.

Strategic premise

AI should return evidence, not define identity.

The system uses semantic retrieval and visible sources to reconnect present questions with earlier traces while preserving the user's interpretive authority.

Problem Framing

I had built an archive I could no longer reach. Years of project notes, personal essays, and unfinished conversation records had accumulated in my Obsidian vault. The problem stopped being capture a long time ago — it became return.

Evidence base
A longitudinal personal archive

Project records, reflections, unfinished writing, and conversation history exposed how ideas recur across different files and years.

Observed breakdown
The memory of a thought is not its filename

What remained available was often a tension, mood, or decision pattern — exactly the material conventional folder and keyword search could not address.

Design constraint
Keep the archive inspectable

AI could mediate access, but the source of record still needed to remain editable, portable, and legible outside the model.

  • Folder search assumes I remember where a thought was filed, not what the thought was
  • Keyword search assumes I remember how I phrased it at the time — often the hardest thing to recall
  • Most of what I retain is shape, not text: a tension, a mood, a decision that felt like an earlier one
  • Even a located record rarely explains its own relationship to the question I'm asking now

Could I retrieve personal experience through meaning rather than location or exact language?

Design Strategy

Turn the archive into a conversational space without turning the model into an authority over the self. When I ask a question, Second Person retrieves conceptually relevant fragments, brings them into the current context, and helps me examine how my thinking has developed over time.

Principle 01
Meaning over exact match

Let vague questions retrieve related experiences even when vocabulary, file location, and original phrasing have changed.

Principle 02
Traceability over fluent authority

Keep the source notes visible so a persuasive response can still be checked against the material it interpreted.

Principle 03
Return over passive storage

Design review, conversation, and idea-development loops that repeatedly bring unfinished material back into use.

Experience Design

Three modes of self-communication — not feature categories, but three different reasons to return to the archive.

Recall
Finding what I could no longer name
  • Starts from a vague question, not a precise keyword
  • Semantic retrieval pulls concept-related records across time and files
  • Answers stay traceable to an identifiable personal source, not generic knowledge
Reflect
Noticing patterns across time
  • A weekly review pulls together the past period's records
  • The system surfaces recurring themes, habits, and tensions
  • The output is a reflection question, not a verdict on who I am
Continue
Letting unfinished thoughts develop
  • A half-formed idea can be logged into the Lab feature
  • Continued dialogue develops a fragment into a clearer idea
  • The fuller version moves into Imagination notes or the personal archive

Implementation Decisions

  • Local-first storage — personal records stay in a vault I can inspect and manage myself
  • Semantic retrieval — relevant records surface without depending on exact keywords
  • Persona file — a written context file gives the model consistent background and interaction rules

Boundary & Governance Decisions

  • Visible sources — responses stay traceable to which personal traces the model was drawing from
  • Continuous indexing — new writing becomes reachable in future retrieval without a manual rebuild
  • Explicit sharing rules — private and family-facing material are separated by an explicit boundary, not a default

Risks, Ethics & Trade-offs

Retrieval is also interpretation. Similarity scores, chunking boundaries, and the model's phrasing all shape which past traces get seen — and how they get connected. The system cannot stand neutral to “the past.”

Stored locally
User-owned records and runtime data

The Obsidian vault, vector indexes, conversation history, reminders, and review cache remain file-based and locally inspectable.

External computation
A local-first system, not a local-only model

Queries and selected context use hosted embedding and language-model APIs; optional web enrichment uses Tavily. That boundary must remain visible rather than hidden behind “local-first.”

Explicit sharing
Public means deliberately marked public

The family experience uses a separate vector index, includes only notes with public: true, and excludes configured folders before indexing.

Coherence vs. Contradiction

Does the AI smooth over a contradictory, uneven history into a more consistent-sounding identity than I actually have?

Convenience vs. Agency

Does lowering retrieval friction also quietly weaken my own memory and my own interpretive authority over my past?

Relevance vs. Serendipity

As retrieval gets more optimized for relevance, does the value of stumbling onto an unrelated, unexpected record get lost?

The system should not produce a definitive version of who I am. It should surface traces, preserve contradiction, and hand back the conditions for meaning — not the meaning itself.

What is proven

  • The end-to-end retrieval, streaming response, source display, capture, and re-indexing loop is implemented
  • The product works across desktop and mobile, with a separate installable family PWA
  • Privacy boundaries are encoded in the indexing architecture rather than left to interface copy alone

What needs validation

  • Evaluate retrieval quality across vague, contradictory, and time-dependent questions
  • Test whether visible sources actually help users challenge confident but incomplete interpretations
  • Study whether repeated AI-mediated recall strengthens reflection or gradually narrows it
  • Threat-model the external model boundary and make context transmission more legible in-product

The goal is not frictionless memory. It is meaningful re-encounter.
