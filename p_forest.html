Fragments of the Forest

A game that teaches ecology by changing who you are — human, squirrel, ant — so children can see the parts of a forest no walk in the woods reveals.

Challenge
Children in exam-driven schools know screens far better than the names of trees or how species depend on each other.
Insight
The parts of a forest that matter most — roots, fungi, scent trails — are invisible to a child on a walk.
Result
A playable three-chapter prototype where each change of scale makes one ecological theory something you do, not read.

Children, parents and teachers all describe the same gap.

The opportunity: show what neither a textbook nor a forest can.

Textbooks are accessible but flat. Real forests are immersive but costly to reach, and still hide their most important layer.

One forest, three bodies, three theories.

Each chapter shrinks the player and swaps in a new way of sensing, so the theory is learned through movement rather than narration.

First Stroke of Water

Human → habitat fragmentation

Follow vanished animal trails and find where roads have cut the forest into islands.

Footnote of Wither

Squirrel → niche & seed dispersal

Swing through the canopy, forage nuts and stock the granary that replants the forest.

Cipher of Scents

Ant → corridors & sensory ecology

Navigate by scent through compound-eye vision to reconnect a dying giant tree.

Built so the theory is something you play.

Built in Unreal Engine 5 Blueprint, with every system serving a learning goal.
