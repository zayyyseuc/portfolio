Silent Signal

A passive headset that watches for the earliest sign of stroke — facial asymmetry — and calls for help when the person wearing it can’t.

Challenge
Older adults living alone often miss stroke’s treatment window because no one notices in time.
Insight
During a stroke you can’t press, speak or remember, so every device that waits for the user fails.
Result
A wearable prototype that detects asymmetry in real time and alerts caregivers with no action from the user.

In stroke care, the delay before anyone notices is what does the damage.

In interviews, people at risk explained why existing devices go unused.

Every product on the market asks the patient to act.

Stroke takes away movement, speech and clear thinking at once. The only reliable trigger is one that needs nothing from the user.

Sense, learn, detect, alert.

Passive sensing

Two EMG electrodes on the cheek muscles. No buttons, no screen.

Personal baseline

Learns the wearer’s own facial symmetry over 72 hours.

Asymmetry detection

A CNN-LSTM model compares both sides of the face in real time.

Silent alert

Caregivers and emergency contacts are notified automatically.

Validated in the lab, accurate enough to justify a field trial.
